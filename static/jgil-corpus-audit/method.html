Method, schema and rubric

How the reports are produced and how to read them.

Pipeline

  1. Extract — src/extract_text.py converts each PDF in pdf/ into page-tagged text (text/<id>.txt) so every claim can cite a PDF page.
  2. Analyse — following docs/ANALYST_GUIDE.md (prompt bundle: src/make_prompt.py), an analyst (here an LLM; in future runs, another LLM) reads the full text and writes json/<id>.json following src/schema/paper.schema.json. Optionally, a corpus synthesis json/_corpus.json (src/schema/corpus.schema.json) records cross-paper findings.
  3. Validate — src/validate.py checks schema conformance and referential integrity (every cited evidence/issue/claim id exists; relations form a graph; thesis present; empirical claims rated adequate+ cite evidence).
  4. Render — src/build_site.py produces this site. Argument maps use Graphviz when available and a built-in layout otherwise.

Access modes

Every record declares how it was produced (document only or web-verified).

  • Document only: only the document is used, and its data are assumed correctly reported. Checks test internal consistency (arithmetic, text vs tables, agreement across documents), not truth against the world. General domain knowledge may appear only as a labelled analyst note.
  • Web-verified: after the document-only analysis, the analyst may consult external sources. These include publisher records (corrections, retractions), cited works (citation checks: does the source say what the paper claims?), original statistics and datasets (external checks, matched on vintage), and replication packages (replication checks). Every source is listed with URL, access date, reliability and version.

The two judgements are kept separate. A claim's support rating always answers "does the paper itself support this?". Its external status (ext: corroborated ext: partially corroborated ext: contradicted ext: not found ext: not checked ) answers "is it consistent with outside evidence?". External findings can change the verdict and the evidential-support score, and the record explains how in an external-verification summary.

What a paper record contains

assessmentEvaluation frame (what standard suits this genre), six rubric scores with rationales, verdict, strengths/weaknesses, robust/fragile claims, what would strengthen the work.

Claim types

  • definitional
  • theoretical
  • analytical
  • empirical descriptive
  • empirical comparative
  • empirical causal
  • interpretive
  • normative
  • predictive
  • methodological
  • background

Definitional, theoretical and normative claims are usually rated not applicable for empirical support; their quality is reflected in conceptual clarity and coherence instead.

Support ratings

strongEvidence directly and convincingly establishes the claim; figures reconcile.
adequateReasonable support for the claim as stated; minor caveats.
partialSupports part of the claim, or the claim is stated more strongly than the evidence allows.
weakEvidence is thin, indirect, or open to clear alternative explanations.
unsupportedNo evidence offered, or evidence contradicts the claim.
not applicableDefinitions, framing premises, normative recommendations.

Obviousness (self-evident / common knowledge / non-trivial / contested) records how much evidence a claim needs: self-evident claims are not penalised for lacking data.

Rubric dimensions (1–5)

conceptual clarityAre key concepts defined, operationalised and used consistently?
logical coherenceDo conclusions follow from premises; are there internal contradictions or gaps?
evidential supportIs the evidence presented adequate, relevant and correctly reported for the claims made?
methodological rigorIs the design appropriate (sampling, measurement, inference) for the questions asked?
transparencyCould a reader reconstruct what was done (data, definitions, procedures, dates, N)?
proportionalityAre conclusions and their certainty proportionate to the evidence (no overreach)?

Scale: 1 = serious deficiencies, 2 = weak, 3 = acceptable with notable problems, 4 = good, 5 = exemplary. Scores are judged relative to the genre stated in each record's evaluation frame (a theoretical essay is not penalised for lacking regressions, but its empirical quantifiers are still checked).

Derived indicators

  • Rubric score = mean of the six dimensions.
  • Claim support index = mean over rated claims of strong 100, adequate 80, partial 50, weak 25, unsupported 0; the core index restricts to thesis/main/conclusion claims.
  • Verdict (analyst judgement): well supported mostly supported partially supported weakly supported unsupported

Issue severity

  • critical invalidates a central conclusion.
  • major substantially weakens a main claim or headline figure.
  • moderate qualifies a claim or its magnitude.
  • minor presentational or peripheral; does not change conclusions.

Limitations of these reports

  • The analyses are LLM-generated and, although grounded in page-cited text and explicit recomputation, can contain misreadings; each claim links to its page for verification.
  • Charts and figures whose values are not printed in the text layer could not be checked.
  • Document-only records cannot detect errors in the underlying data or misrepresented citations; web-verified records can, but only for what the analyst checked (see each record's access notes).
  • Ratings are judgements; the rationales are the substantive content and should be read alongside the scores.
