Summary

After outlining Spain's homeownership-centred housing model, the crash, SAREB and the PAH's repertoire, the paper describes each film and compares their strategies. Seven Days (made by PAH insiders, Rosa Luxemburg-funded) is a fast-paced, pedagogical 'militant' manual that explains the PAH model and the macro context; Mortgaged Lives and Dignity (Michelle Teran, direct-cinema 'fly on the wall') foreground intimate, intersectional micro-experiences of mostly migrant working-class women, care and squatting, but reached tiny audiences (~500 online views each); The Divide combines direct cinema with contextual 'lecturing' (real-estate fairs, investor forums, court scenes) and circulated widely in festivals and PAH groups. The authors conclude that the films complement each other: Seven Days works as an organising toolbox, the others generate empathy among broader audiences, and filmmakers' activist engagement shaped production and dissemination.

Research questions

  1. How do the documentaries differ in representing the context of housing struggles?
  2. What is the underlying narrative strategy of each film?
  3. To what extent do these films contribute to housing activism in Spain and elsewhere?

Stated contribution

A comparative typology of activist housing documentaries and an assessment of their contributions to a housing movement.

How the argument is built

Visibility in social movements → Spanish housing crisis and PAH → description of the four films → comparison informed by filmmaker interviews → typology (two axes) and efficacy assessment.

Evidence & data

Design

Qualitative. Comparative case study of four purposively selected films; filmmaker interviews plus interpretive socio-semiotic analysis.

Scope: Spain, films produced 2012-2017.

Methods

  • Narrative description of films
  • Socio-semiotic interpretation
  • Thematic use of filmmaker interviews
  • Comparative typology

Transparency notes

Film selection criteria stated; interviewees listed in footnotes; no interview protocol, no systematic coding of film content, no audience/reception data for the two films judged most effective.

Limitations acknowledged by the authors

  • Documentaries are not scientific works seeking accurate sampling or causal tests.
  • Selection restricted to films by PAH-engaged directors.

Overall assessment

Evaluation frame: An interpretive media analysis of a small purposive set of films. Appropriate standards: clarity and usefulness of the typology, faithfulness of film descriptions, grounding of claims about production in interviews, and caution in claims about reception/impact.
