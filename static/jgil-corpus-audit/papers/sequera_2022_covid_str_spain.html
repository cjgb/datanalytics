Summary

After outlining Spain's tourism dependence and a decade of touristification, the authors (members of the LIKEALOCAL project) note that Airbnb bookings collapsed in 2020 and many tourist flats appeared on residential portals: in central Madrid 1,796 listings were recorded, 73.61% estimated to be former tourist apartments; long-term listings in the Central District went from 1,300 (2019) to 4,000 (May 2020) and 4,600 (May 2021), then back to 1,900 (Sept 2021). They argue this was not a return to residential use but a temporary switch via 'seasonal' contracts that border on illegality, that professional managers were strengthened while small ones were expelled, and that platforms consolidated as infrastructures enabling hyper-commodification by blurring tourist and residential markets and by creating new demand (digital nomads, students, expats). They close with a research agenda on tourism-dependent economies.

Research questions

  1. How did COVID-19 shift STR supply and the type of demand in Spain?
  2. What changes might the pandemic bring to the interplay between the 'classical' real estate market and STRs?

Stated contribution

Theoretical reflections grounded in the LIKEALOCAL project's empirical work, proposing flexibilization/hybridisation of housing markets via platforms as the key pandemic legacy.

How the argument is built

Tourism dependence → prior touristification research (project members' studies) → STR model and rent gap → pandemic switch of listings to residential portals → interpretation: temporary, seasonal contracts, professionalisation, infrastructures of flexibility → platforms as market creators → research agenda.

Links to the rest of the corpus

X1 contradiction moderate Did pandemic STRs return to the residential market?

Gil (2023) states that during the pandemic 'STRs did not return to the residential market (Gil et al., 2023)' and uses this to argue for housing-policy (not tourism-policy) responses. Gil, Vidal & Yrigoy (2026) cite the same source for STR units being 'temporarily redirected to residential or mid-term segments'. Sequera et al. (2022) document central Madrid long-term listings rising from 1,300 to 4,600 and back to 1,900, and Gil (2021) reported hosts 'directly ending the business and renting in the residential market'. The evidence supports a temporary switch, largely under seasonal contracts; the 2023 paper's unqualified statement understates it.

Implication: The policy argument in Gil (2023) should be qualified: switching occurred but was temporary and often outside long-term residential contracts.

X12 corroboration positive Later data temper earlier demand-side claim

Sequera et al. (2022) asserted that STR continuity rested on a demand shift to digital nomads, students and expats. Gil, Vidal & Yrigoy (2026), with Idealista search data, find foreign demand is not a significant driver in multivariate models (an 'enabler'), while confirming the seasonal-contract mechanism.

Implication: Shows the programme updating claims with better data.
